## Research Position

TradeGuard Shield is fundamentally a **research and engineering project**, not a commercial verification service or regulatory authority.

Its primary objective is to investigate whether heterogeneous public signals—including domain registration metadata, phishing intelligence, transport security observations, content-based indicators, and limited regulatory evidence—can be combined into an explainable and operationally safe risk-intelligence framework.

The methodological position of the author is deliberately conservative:

- evidence should always be distinguishable from interpretation;
- uncertainty should remain visible rather than being hidden by deterministic scores;
- external data sources should degrade gracefully under failure conditions;
- every assessment should preserve the possibility of correction, appeal, and independent verification.

Accordingly, TradeGuard Shield does **not** determine whether an organisation is fraudulent, licensed, trustworthy, solvent, or legally compliant. It produces transparent indicators intended to support human judgement, investigative triage, and further due diligence rather than replacing professional legal, financial, or regulatory evaluation.

The broader research agenda includes explainable scoring models, calibration of decision thresholds, empirical precision and recall analysis, reproducibility, operational resilience, and responsible deployment of public-interest cybersecurity tools.

## Academic Principles

The project follows several core scientific and engineering principles:

- **Transparency** — scoring logic, assumptions, and evidence sources are explicitly documented whenever possible.
- **Reproducibility** — methodologies are designed to be independently reviewed, tested, and challenged.
- **Fail-Neutral Architecture** — unavailable external providers produce unknown or neutral outcomes instead of fabricated conclusions.
- **Source Attribution** — observable evidence is preferred over opaque proprietary reasoning.
- **Ethical Responsibility** — risk indicators must never be presented as accusations or judicial determinations.

These principles are intended to encourage constructive peer review and independent evaluation rather than unquestioned acceptance of automated outputs.
